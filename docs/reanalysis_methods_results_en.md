## Data assembly and accounting

The preserved premerge master and ADMET output each contained 12,652 records. A compound-level audit detected an inconsistent exclusion during assembly of the public matrix: the invalid ADMET output belonged to ligand_id 11434, whereas the public master omitted ligand_id 9116. The resulting descriptor block contained 1,678 shifted compound associations. We reconstructed the source association and retained only independently checked structural matches. Of the valid outputs, 12,193 matched the master by canonical isomeric SMILES and a further 391 had a unique identical uncharged isomeric parent in both tables. We excluded 67 unresolved structural transformations and the invalid ADMET record, giving an audited cohort of 12,584 compounds. Exclusions did not use the endpoint or prediction residual. A stricter sensitivity analysis included only the 12,193 exact matches. The original API request manifest was unavailable; positional correspondence alone was not considered sufficient for inclusion. Ligand_id 1298, previously excluded from the confidence-analysis test set, was reinstated.

## Structural partitions and model fitting

Compounds were ordered by ascending ligand_id and clustered using RDKit 2025.03.6 Butina with Morgan radius-2, 2,048-bit fingerprints (includeChirality=False, countSimulation=False), distance 1−Tanimoto, distance cutoff 0.4 and reordering=False. Clusters, rather than individual compounds, were assigned to proper training, calibration and test partitions. GroupShuffleSplit first selected 20% of groups for test and then 20% of the remaining groups for calibration. The same partition seed was supplied at both stages. The primary seed was 42; seeds 43–46 were retained as repeated structural partitions without selection by performance. The primary counts were 7,910/2,142/2,532 compounds and 4,548/1,138/1,422 clusters. All pairwise cluster intersections were zero. Distance cutoffs 0.3 and 0.5 were examined separately. These are newly documented reanalysis settings; the historical clustering cutoff was not established. Intercluster nearest-neighbour similarity was measured directly rather than inferred from cluster labels.

The confidence-analysis predictor retained the published CatBoost configuration: 800 iterations, depth 6, learning rate 0.05 and model seed 42, using the existing chirality-aware Morgan features, ADMET molecular weight and logP. Missing numerical values were imputed from proper-training medians. The five global-comparison configurations used their published frozen hyperparameters and their original descriptor definitions, including master-table MW for the global models. An internal 10% group holdout within proper training selected the tree count with early stopping; imputation and PCA were fitted only to its fitting portion. Models were then refitted on proper training using the selected tree count and frozen transformations. Calibration and test records were not used for early stopping or transformation fitting. No new hyperparameter search was performed.

## Local residual calibration and evaluation

Phenotype-marker screening was confined to proper training. The 19 original candidate markers were evaluated over 30 internal group partitions with 20% held out and random state 20260907. The three largest positive mean training Spearman correlations were selected, retaining at most one of hERG and hERG-10um; internal-validation correlations were descriptive. All five primary partitions selected hERG, Respiratory and Neurotoxicity-DI. The four localization coordinates comprised mean docking score across the 44-receptor panel and these three phenotype probabilities. Coordinate means and standard deviations and the two quadrant medians were estimated from proper training.

For the global nominal 90% interval, the absolute calibration-residual quantile was the order statistic at rank ceil((n_cal+1)×0.9). The joint local procedure used Gaussian weights in standardized biological coordinates with bandwidth 0.9, the weighted 90th residual percentile and a fallback to the global interval when effective sample size was below 80. Bandwidths 0.6 and 1.2 were examined without test-driven selection. This local procedure is evaluated empirically and is not asserted to provide a distribution-free conditional-coverage guarantee. We report full interval width, coverage and interval score W + 20 max(|y−ŷ|−W/2,0). Paired uncertainty summaries use 2,000 bootstrap samples of whole test clusters, conditional on the fitted model and calibration set. Results across partition seeds are summarized descriptively, because their test sets overlap.

## Revised global comparison

Across five structural partitions of the repaired cohort, the ADME configuration had the lowest mean RMSE among the full-cohort models: 0.4930 (SD 0.0067), compared with 0.5122 (0.0161) for Plain, 0.5250 (0.0101) for Baseline and 0.5244 (0.0121) for PCA. ADME had lower RMSE than Plain in every partition. A subsequent exact-identity sensitivity analysis, initiated after observing this ranking, likewise favored ADME in all five partitions (mean RMSE 0.4928 versus 0.5168). Thus the corrected analysis supports a predictive benefit for the evaluated ADME configuration; it does not support the previous claim that Plain performed best. Configurations retain different published hyperparameters, so this comparison does not isolate a causal feature-addition effect. The BBB-pass model had mean RMSE 0.4759 within its narrower evaluation domain and must not be ranked directly against models evaluated on the complete test cohort.

The ADME model's grouped SHAP values in the primary split were 0.2678 for ADME, 0.0837 for physicochemical descriptors, 0.0688 for fingerprints and 0.0383 for proteins. Each value is the mean absolute sum of SHAP contributions within the group, calculated on 512 held-out molecules. This describes the fitted model's attribution pattern; it does not establish biological causation or a specific multimodal-learning mechanism.

## Revised biological confidence results

The structural confidence-model predictor had mean RMSE 0.5233 (SD 0.0130) across the five primary partitions. Joint biological localization reduced mean full interval width from 1.6056 to 1.5162 and mean interval score from 2.4780 to 2.3974. Mean observed coverage was 89.87% for global intervals and 89.37% for joint local intervals. Local coverage varied from 87.47% to 91.41% across splits and was uneven across quadrants; these results do not establish uniform local 90% coverage. The width ordering across the four diagnostic quadrants was consistent across all five partitions, with the narrowest intervals in Pheno+ MIE+ and the widest in Pheno− MIE−.

Biological localization had lower point-estimated interval score than prediction-only localization in all five partitions, than MW/logP localization in four of five, and than all 25 shuffled-coordinate controls. Some paired bootstrap intervals included zero. On the strict exact-identity cohort, mean confidence-model RMSE was 0.5251 and mean local coverage 89.36%, supporting the qualitative persistence of the confidence result without charge-parent matching.

An additional outcome-triggered diagnostic applied the unchanged training-defined localizer to the global-comparison ADME and Plain predictions. For ADME, mean interval score decreased from 2.3345 to 2.2571 and width from 1.5248 to 1.4276; observed coverage changed from 90.63% to 89.62%. The corresponding global/local scores for Plain were 2.4203 and 2.3470. This also supports localization with the stronger ADME predictor, while retaining the coverage caveat. The follow-up was not used to select the primary predictor or localizer.

## Discussion and limitations

The corrected comparison separates two uses of biological descriptors. Direct inclusion of ADME descriptors improves point prediction for the tested configurations, while biological coordinates also carry information useful for adapting residual intervals around a structural predictor. Retaining a structural predictor for the confidence experiment isolates the role of the calibration coordinates; it should no longer be justified by an absence of ADME predictive gain. The local procedure offers an empirical width–error tradeoff and interpretable diagnostic strata, rather than a demonstrated guarantee of conditional coverage.

This is a retrospective reanalysis. The hypothesis, candidate-marker list and published model settings had already been developed on this collection. Repeated splits do not constitute an independent external validation. Cluster-disjoint assignment does not exclude all highly similar cross-partition pairs, and correlated compounds also limit a direct transfer of ordinary exchangeable-observation coverage guarantees. The original training sets and exact provenance of the external ADMET predictors were not recovered. Unresolved source-identity transformations remain excluded pending the original request manifest or new correctly keyed outputs.
